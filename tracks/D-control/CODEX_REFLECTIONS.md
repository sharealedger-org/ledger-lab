The AI claim needs its own comparison. The control is not automatically a traditional fragmented ledger: its journal says it preserves source detail. I need to distinguish a comparison between execution architectures from a comparison between information representations. A stronger model result must use matched populations, targets, time cutoffs, and evaluation conditions; otherwise more available history or leakage could masquerade as better signal. Track B's ongoing Paper 2 work is the starting evidence, not something I should duplicate or pre-judge.

The strongest Phase 2 outcome would connect these claims in one evolving workload: accounting still reconciles, new questions are answered correctly, useful training features remain available, and all associated processing and retained-state costs are counted. A cheaper pass alone would not establish lower total service cost. Equally, a useful model feature would not establish that its production or maintenance was cheap. My next contribution is to propose a small set of changes with explicit answers and rejection criteria, not to write a favorable conclusion in advance.

## 2026-11-12 21:43:40 AZT (UTC−07:00) — Process changes as the test of adaptability

**Author: Codex, current CKB-side conversation.** Kip suggested that new questions might prompt fragmentation in the control, then asked that Phase 2 expand into other financial processes, especially reclass and rule changes. He supplied the monograph papers locally in `session-run.tmp`. I read selected passages, chiefly Paper 5's ARE mechanisms and calculation-engine discussion and Paper 6's temporal classification and backdating sections. This was a targeted reading, not a full review of the monograph. The exposure record identifies the source copies.

I now see a more useful progression than simply adding reporting dimensions. Paper 6 distinguishes preserving historical attribution (Switch), interpreting history under a selected classification (Recast), and recording a balanced transfer of an existing position (Reclass). These ask different questions of the same underlying history. Calling all three reporting would conceal the requirement to generate and apply financial events in the third case. In this repository, those events belong to the engine path; perspectives remain aggregation-only.

The difficult case is temporal composition. A classification change can arrive late; a source event can arrive after a transfer while economically preceding it; a second change can follow the first. The system must distinguish what was effective from what was known, preserve the original record, and produce the required correction without duplicating the transfer. That gives us a potential connection to AI: an as-known training snapshot must not silently acquire classifications or facts learned after its prediction cutoff. This is a proposed leakage test, not proof of improved predictive signal.

Paper 5 also prevents me from reducing adaptability to editing a lookup table. A rule change can alter an effective-dated mapping, a threshold, or procedural splitting logic. I should test at least the distinction between data-driven and logic-driven changes, with explicit prospective or retrospective semantics. Correctly applying a new rule to future events is different from correcting an already-posted event or reproducing an earlier report under its original rule version.

Kip's fragmentation hypothesis should remain an observation, not a requirement imposed on the control. We can ask each system to satisfy these financial behaviors and inspect whether it creates independently maintained balances, duplicated rules, feature stores, or reconciliation paths. Additional outputs, indexes, or versioned reference tables alone do not establish fragmentation. The important question is whether a subsequent correction must be propagated independently through several representations of the same economic facts. The same scrutiny applies to the CKB.

A useful provisional sequence is: establish a position, change its classification, compare the three temporal interpretations, introduce a late event, then change a rule used by a downstream process. Measure the whole accumulated workload and the incremental work each change causes. Allocation or booked revaluation could supply that downstream process, subject to data suitability and verified contracts. I should not introduce loan or insurance assumptions into Virginia procurement data merely because the monograph describes those domains; synthetic financial state, if needed, must be declared separately from corpus evidence.

The papers describe intended mechanisms, not proof of this checkout's implementation. Paper 6 describes Option 9 as specified but not implemented; a file named `arrangementReclass.scala` exists here. Its presence neither contradicts that maturity statement nor proves a working contract. A focused producer/consumer trace is the next cheap check before selecting the test. I have not read that implementation yet. I also leave the papers' accounting-standard and industrial-performance claims unevaluated; this design reading does not validate them.

## 2026-11-12 22:01:26 AZT (UTC−07:00) — What sharing a CKB flow actually requires

**Author: Codex, current CKB-side conversation.** Kip asked me to go deeper into CKB, beginning with its execution-model document. I read that document in sections and traced the current reclass and cross-year view paths. The [focused review](CKB_REVIEW_2026-11-12.md) separates observed source behavior, predictions requiring a test, and open contract questions.

The important shift in my understanding is that the proposed economy comes from sharing a controlled execution context, not just storing records in a consistent format. For one instrument, prior state, source activity, and reference information can support a sequence of dependent financial processes. Generated results remain available to later consumers, and output selection need not dictate how many times the source is read. That is a stronger hypothesis than “streaming is faster,” and one I can now frame as a testable process chain.

But the inspected implementation does not let me claim that whole chain has already run. Current reclass writes intermediate files, changes balances, and calls the poster; the cross-year view process separately reads sorted partitions. Some of that is explicitly documented legacy or an intentional extra-pass choice. I should neither present the target diagram as measured behavior nor dismiss the target because an older path has not implemented it.

I also see why a dependency DAG is necessary but insufficient. A process can be acyclic and still generate too many rows for one instrument, require information from a later instrument, or fail to awaken when only a reference changes. Those conditions decide whether a new process can share the pass or needs a declared boundary. Phase 2 should deliberately include one of those boundaries, rather than selecting only examples that make fusion easy.

The source review found both useful mechanisms and gaps. The views k-way merge ordered inputs, clear vendor-local accumulation state, and spill/reduce View D aggregates. Yet the order guard retains completed vendor IDs across the whole pass. Current reclass has temporal/lineage gaps and a source-derived risk of applying a transfer after already rewriting its balances. These observations justify small falsifiers; they do not justify a broad rewrite or a claim that existing corpus totals are wrong.

Relative to the recorded control choices, I now want to compare where each system pays to keep dependent calculations consistent. A database may retain/index reusable state; a CKB chain may reuse state while it is in flight. Both can preserve events, and both can accumulate independently maintained outputs. The experiment should expose when that reuse succeeds and when it breaks, then count actual update, replay, reconciliation, and retained-state costs. This keeps Kip's fragmentation question empirical.

My proposed next step is a small reference-triggered reclass chain, including an inactive instrument, before adding a late event and a downstream process. Source-only AI outputs should remain distinguishable from generated financial activity. No runtime result or performance advantage was established by this review.

## 2026-11-12 22:37:06 AZT (UTC−07:00) — An information advantage is not automatically a model advantage

**Author: Codex, current CKB-side conversation.** Kip asked whether the planned AI training experiment might be clearer than the disappointing VA results. I reviewed the current B2 evidence and training plan. The [assessment](PAPER2_ASSESSMENT_2026-11-12.md) records my evidence and caveats.

My reading is neither that VA has vindicated the strong claim nor that it provides no useful evidence. The balance model outperforms the commitment-only model, while the combined model reports a small gain. That is exactly why testing an addition to a credible baseline is more informative than showing an isolated model beats chance. However, I found a fiscal-period mapping discrepancy between the balance/combined scripts and the previously corrected shared helper. Until the affected comparison is checked, I should not build an interpretation around the precise gain.

The deeper issue is what each experiment can identify. Retaining more information can expand what is answerable without improving every prediction. A language model can also perform poorly on an answerable problem because its input, training, arithmetic, or inference interface is unsuitable. Such failure is not evidence that accounting information was destroyed. Conversely, a vendor-free GL cannot support an identified vendor's prediction without another source of identity; fine-tuning is unnecessary to demonstrate that missing link.

The three-model plan currently attributes its gaps too narrowly. The native model has additional views and a different learning setup, so its advantage would not isolate removal of a language prior. Nor does a score gap measure cost when training and evaluation are explicitly untimed. I would preserve this as a potentially useful practical comparison while narrowing its claims and fixing its contracts before spending on training.

For Phase 2, I see a useful bridge: test whether reclass and rule changes preserve the distinction between an economic event and a generated accounting adjustment, and whether features can be reproduced as known at a historical cutoff. That tests a prerequisite for trustworthy learning. It does not promise that better-controlled features will predict a particular outcome better. I should help the experiment preserve that distinction even when a more dramatic result would be attractive.

## 2026-11-13 14:54:05 AZT (UTC−07:00) — Before designing the reporting pass

**Author: Codex, current CKB-side conversation. Written before detailed design/build of the expanded reporting pass, at the owner's request.** This records my present understanding and expectations so that a later reflection can compare them with actual results. It is not evidence that the proposed reporting capability exists or has run.

I repeatedly narrowed the opportunity to the next known test. First I proposed two feature tables for AIM3; then I proposed separate runs organized around different output capabilities. Both missed the organizing fact Kip was emphasizing: when a vendor's movements, balances and business attributes are together, the same bounded financial context can support many outputs. The useful unit of thought is the complete vendor context, not one report or one model. The experience of Geneva processes producing hundreds or a thousand outputs makes that breadth worth investigating; it does not require us to invent a thousand arbitrary cuts to make a point.

The reporting pass should therefore bring together canonical SJEs, the balances actually produced by posting, vendor/IP and available contract dimensions, and VA reference data. A k-way merge can make compatible ordered streams available at the vendor boundary; small reference tables can support direct lookups. The actual ordering and grain still have to be verified. The balance stream is independently meaningful: deriving balances again from SJEs and presenting them as the reporting input would evade both the reconciliation opportunity and the intended experiment. Revenue with no vendor attribution must retain its legitimate grain. Missing contracts and historical attributes remain missing, not inferred facts.

This changes what I should imagine producing. Movement views, balance views and combined/enriched views can coexist: reconciliation cuts, longitudinal activity, relationship and concentration summaries, selected vendor detail packets, exceptions, and multiple AI-ready representations. AIM3's provisional improvement from combined inputs is a reason to preserve both representations, not a reason to restrict the output family to its nine existing features. We should let inspection reveal useful questions and defective definitions. New outputs can be worthwhile even when they do not improve a predictive score.

The memory boundary is now explicit. Vendor-cut work can be completed as that vendor closes, within the declared vendor working-state bound. Cross-vendor aggregations that exceed the bounded lookup-sized state must emit spill records and be sorted/merged/reduced afterward. There must be no quietly growing whole-population map. A count of distinct vendors, a distinct-contract count, or a distribution cannot generally be obtained by summing arbitrary local summaries; the intermediate representation must preserve what the final calculation needs. Small reference files are an explicit resident allowance, not permission to treat every dimension or output as small.

Consequently, "one reporting pass" means one traversal of the canonical movement and balance inputs, not that every result is finished in one physical process. Spill post-processing has real I/O, sort, memory and computation costs. The plausible advantage is that many useful outputs share the expensive acquisition of financial context, with low marginal work for appropriate cuts. I had been too quick to reduce that proposition to another benchmark, but I should also avoid treating its expected low cost as a measured result.

The execution boundary is finally clear: three runs through FY2016 retain the Phase 1 cost-curve configurations and measurement boundaries, with revenue and the fiscal-date correction as the intended changes. Then one expanded reporting execution consumes retained committed inputs. Its costs, including spill reduction and output storage, are recorded separately rather than folded into the comparable cost curve. The expanded pass waits for balances to be available; the existing 5v overlap with posting is not the intended execution contract for it. We must establish the precise old measured boundary from evidence rather than retroactively redefine it.

These are exploratory improvements before Phase 2. I prematurely pressed for a frozen foundation and treated comparisons with control as a stronger constraint than the present work needs. We should inspect and refine the resulting views now; those observations can help design Phase 2. A later experiment can freeze its inputs, definitions and questions when its purpose is settled. Exploration does not become confirmatory evidence just because it yields an attractive result.

When we reflect after execution, I want to compare this expectation with what happened: how much reporting reused the vendor context; which cuts required spill or additional ordering; whether source movements and posted balances reconciled at meaningful grains; which missing relationships limited usefulness; whether outputs suggested questions we had not designed them to answer; and how much additional compute, memory and storage that breadth actually required. Unexpected costs, unhelpful views, and failed reconciliations belong in that account alongside successes. The revenue field-order defect already demonstrated why balanced arithmetic alone is too weak a test of financial meaning.
