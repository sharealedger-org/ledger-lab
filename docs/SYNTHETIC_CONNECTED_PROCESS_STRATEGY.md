## Phase 2: vendor-centered evidence for early AI work

The [Phase 2 generation CKB proposal](PHASE_TWO_GENERATION_CKB_PROPOSAL.md)
specifies a proposed context-order pass followed by an entity-temporal pass,
with source policy, record families, state limits and acceptance gates. It is
pending design discussion before test implementation.

Extend vendor procurement, payment and expenditure histories with limited revenue
and employee populations. Preserve their distinct identities, record grains and
business-process semantics. Connections may be recorded relationships or evidence
of possible relationships through organizational responsibility, projects,
locations and dates. Shared attributes alone do not establish a connection.
Allocation journals are not required to expose or investigate these relationships.

Prepare a scenario matrix before revising generation code. For each case record:

| Contract | Required content |
|---|---|
| Processes and records | Populations, grain, source/synthetic provenance and parent lineage |
| Identity and temporal state | Namespaces, CKB keys, ordering, effective dates and observation cutoff |
| Visible evidence | Records and attributes available to the AI at the cutoff |
| Withheld generation truth | Intended relationship, generation mechanism and assumptions |
| Inferability | Whether visible evidence supports a unique answer, several candidates or no conclusion |
| Controls | Expected totals, balancing where applicable, temporal joins and downstream consumers |

Include explicit links, indirect corroboration, ambiguous alternatives,
coincidental similarities, contradictory evidence and unrelated controls. Keep
ordinary records free of answer-bearing scenario identifiers. Generation truth
and validation oracles remain outside AI-visible inputs. An intended synthetic
relationship may be unidentifiable from the supplied evidence; evaluation must
allow that distinction and reward justified uncertainty.

Tactical sequence:

1. Map scenarios to the actual Papers 3 and 4 outline and agree the initial populations.
2. Declare producer/consumer contracts, CKB placement, temporal operands, sorting,
   materialization bounds and controls before implementing the revised generator.
3. Generate a small deterministic connected fixture and evidence views. Retain
   observed VA source semantics; generated detail is explicitly synthetic.
4. Complete the latest streamed-assignment reporting end-to-end verification;
   run unchanged baseline and named connected-workload checks.
5. Freeze input versions, seeds, rules, assumptions and evaluation cases; execute
   and inspect one new full corpus run after admission checks pass.
6. Begin AI relationship experiments using the validated outputs and named
   evaluators, without waiting for allocation/reclass engine construction.

No model effectiveness is established by this plan. Trace features and evidence
to their actual evaluator; report synthetic findings as synthetic findings.

## Bias controls across both stages

A vendor-first design risks embedding the eventual answer in the generator.
Document that limitation rather than assuming a withheld label removes it.
Use more than one relationship mechanism and preserve competing explanations,
missing evidence and negative cases. Avoid making generated vendor attributes
deterministic proxies for relationship labels. Apply temporal cutoffs so later
events and reference knowledge do not leak into earlier predictions.

Freeze generation and evaluation specifications before evaluating models. Reserve
independently specified scenario families for holdout evaluation, including cases
outside the initial vendor-centered topology. Compare results across seeds,
relationship mechanisms and evidence availability as later experimental scope
permits. Record any post-result generator changes as new versions; do not silently
tune the evaluation population to favor the proposed architecture or AI outcome.

## Phase 3: broader process topology and tentative connections

**Owner extension, 2026-11-15:** Generation contexts may represent invented
business components spanning conventional processes. Emit proposed activity from
those components into the process CKB flows. Keep this independent and recognizable
from observed VA data. Where feasible, bound generation with compatible published
VA financial anchors and explicit adjustment bridges. Separate supported
adjustments, synthetic calibration and unexplained residuals; avoid overlapping
observed/synthetic coverage. See the generation proposal's financial bridge section.

Describe a graph of business processes with declared inputs, state, outputs and
consumers. Each process has the CKB key appropriate to its workload; a common key
can be any set of attributes. Multiple business processes require declared CKB
flows, not an automatic physical scan for each logical layer or view. Required
sort changes and unbounded materialization determine physical pass boundaries.

Allocations connect separate business processes through rules proposing how they
might relate. They remain tentative even when execution balances exactly. Record
the proposal, evidence, assumptions and alternatives separately from any accepted
processing rule. A proposal should specify source/destination populations,
matching attributes, basis, weights, effective dates, version and applicability.
Financial allocation processing consumes declared inputs and emits balanced SJEs;
destination processes must demonstrably consume those outputs. Perspectives
aggregate results and do not generate accounting transactions.

Future-dated reclass supports temporary outcomes under proposed reference data,
hierarchy or rule changes. Generate reclass journals within the temporal CKB pass
for the relevant vendor, employee, taxpayer or other entity. It is not a detached
reclass pass unrelated to entity state. Preserve GenevaERS effective-date lookup
semantics: reference versions are selected by declared date operands; this does
not mean snapshotting the whole database. Explicit scenario/run dates make
experiments reproducible.

Temporary scenario state and outputs need scenario identity, parent baseline,
reference/rule versions, effective dates, lineage and controls. Compare baseline
and proposed outcomes, including effects propagated through declared process
connections. Do not merge temporary results into accepted ledger state by
implication. Dependency ordering must follow actual consumers, including any
allocation/reclass interactions; repeated processing requires explicit termination
and reconciliation contracts. These are future design requirements, not existing
runtime capabilities.

## Longer-term AI vision

Use a repeatable loop: expose evidence; propose relationships, allocation rules or
reference changes; validate the proposal's contracts; execute a controlled
temporary scenario; inspect reconciled differences and competing explanations.
AI can begin with evidence retrieval, candidate linkage, explanation and calibrated
uncertainty in Phase 2. Phase 3 extends that work to executable rule proposals and
counterfactual outcomes.

Keep three judgments separate: whether a relationship is supported by evidence,
whether a rule is admissible under declared contracts, and what outcome results
when it executes. Balanced journals establish accounting controls, not business
truth, causal validity or correctness of a proposed allocation. Future-dated
outputs are conditional scenarios, not forecasts established by execution alone.
